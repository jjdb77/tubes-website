Channel 4 will not bring Married at First Sight UK back to screens. Chief executive Priya Dogra announced the decision on Thursday following an external review of participant care and the handling of previous complaints.

The decision also applies to the sixth season, which had already been filmed earlier this year. According to Sky News, Channel 4 confirmed that it would not broadcast the season. The broadcaster is therefore not only ending a long-running format, but also accepting a financial loss on an already produced series.

The external review followed serious allegations of misconduct involving a small number of former participants being put to the broadcaster. Channel 4 stresses that the review did not establish whether those allegations were substantiated. The participants concerned denied the allegations, the broadcaster said.

The investigation consisted of a confidential legal review by Clyde & Co and a separate evaluation led by Lorraine Heggessey, former controller of BBC One. According to RTÉ, the second review examined, among other things, how the format could place pressure on participants and the existing welfare protocols.

For producer CPL Productions, the decision means losing a key commissioner. Channel 4 says it wants to redirect the Married at First Sight UK budget into distinctive new programmes. The broadcaster also says it will publish a summary of the recommendations and share it with other British broadcasters.

The case also raises questions about oversight of reality production. Ofcom opened an investigation into the programme in July following a complaint about fairness and privacy. The regulator has not thereby ruled on the substantive allegations, but the issue makes clear that welfare, informed consent and oversight during production are carrying increasing weight in the commissioning process.

The decision shows that even an already filmed season can still be dropped. For producers, that may be a reason to review their care procedures, independent support and ways of reporting problems on set.
