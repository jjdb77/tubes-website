France tv distribution has launched Creator’s Hub, a label for new programmes developed directly for YouTube, Facebook and Snapchat. The initiative aims to connect television talent with creators who already have an online audience.

Creator’s Hub is being set up by France tv distribution, the distribution arm of the French public service broadcaster France Télévisions. According to the announcement, it is not about republishing existing television programmes, but about formats designed from the outset for social platforms and their usage patterns.

The label aims to connect France Télévisions personalities with creators who have an existing audience on YouTube and other social services. In doing so, the group is seeking to bring together two forms of audience relationship: the recognisability and production infrastructure of a broadcaster on the one hand, and the direct distribution and community-building of online creators on the other.

The commercial and organisational set-up is broader than a talent programme. France tv distribution cites collaboration with FranceTV Publicité, the group’s advertising arm, and france.tv studio, its production unit. This points to a model in which development, production, audience reach and commercial exploitation are connected within the same group.

For producers, the format logic is particularly relevant. The programmes will not necessarily be made first for a linear channel and then carried over digitally. The chosen approach starts with the platforms themselves, with their own lengths, storytelling forms and consumption patterns. This could have implications for rights packages, brand safety, sponsor integration, moderation and the division of revenues between broadcaster, creator and platform.

France Télévisions has not yet published a list of the first programmes, creators, budgets or contract terms. Nor is it known exactly what role France tv distribution will have in the international sale or adaptation of the new formats. The announcement does make clear that the group sees social video not only as an additional form of reach, but as a separate development environment.

The move is part of a broader European competition for young audiences and online viewing time. Public service broadcasters must balance their public remit with the logic of commercial platforms. Creator’s Hub gives France Télévisions a way to organise that logic internally, but its success may depend in part on whether creators are willing to share their audiences and whether the formats can be exploited beyond a single platform.

For the market, the first signal is primarily institutional: a public service broadcasting group is building a recognisable label around creator collaboration, rather than running separate pilots for each channel. Budgets, rights agreements and initial results will help assess how the label works in practice.
