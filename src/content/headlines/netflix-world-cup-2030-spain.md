Netflix has bought the exclusive broadcasting rights to the 2030 FIFA World Cup in Spain and Andorra. The streaming service plans to show all 104 matches live, while some matches will also be available via a subscription-free broadcaster.

Netflix announced on Tuesday that it had acquired the exclusive rights in Spain and Andorra. The agreement covers the entire tournament, including the opening matches and the final. The financial value of the agreement has not been disclosed.

The streaming service says it plans to broadcast all the matches live. There will also be Spanish-language previews and reviews, programmes around the matches and a daily round-up on matchdays. Netflix is thereby positioning itself not only as a distributor, but also as a producer of local sports programmes.

The exclusivity has an important limitation. In Spain, at least one match will be offered free of charge on every matchday. All Spain matches, the semi-finals and the final must also be made available through a national broadcaster without a paywall. Which broadcaster that will be has not yet been announced.

The 2030 World Cup will be hosted by Spain, Portugal and Morocco. To mark the tournament’s centenary, matches will also be played in Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay. The main tournament will then move to the Iberian Peninsula and North Africa.

For Netflix, the purchase is a new step into live sport. The service already holds the rights to the 2027 and 2031 Women’s World Cups in the United States and Canada, but the men’s World Cup will be a far more visible test of its technical capacity and consumers’ willingness to follow sport via streaming.

The agreement does not affect broadcasting rights in the Netherlands. These are allocated separately for each market. Dutch viewers therefore cannot draw any conclusions from the Spanish agreement about how the World Cup will be shown here. The purchase does, however, underline the growing competition between streaming services and traditional broadcasters for major sporting events.
