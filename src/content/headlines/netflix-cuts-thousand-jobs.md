Netflix is cutting around 1,000 jobs, the streaming service has reported according to several public news sources. The redundancies affect around 6 per cent of its global workforce and represent the company’s largest round since 2022.

The jobs are being cut across various parts of Netflix. According to reports based on an internal email from co-chief executives Greg Peters and Ted Sarandos, teams working on product development, technology, legal affairs and human resources are being hit hardest.

Netflix informed employees of the redundancies on Wednesday. The precise number of roles affected in each country and department has not been made public. Nor is it known how many employees in Europe or the Netherlands will be affected. Netflix did not immediately respond to questions from Reuters, the Irish public broadcaster RTÉ reported.

The scale of the measure is notable because, according to the available reports, it brings the company to a level it has not reached since 2022. The sources do not establish a complete reason for the redundancies. Nor has it been disclosed whether the measure is linked to a change in the offering, prices or international production.

The reorganisation could have a direct impact on some employees outside the United States. Netflix has offices and production teams worldwide, but the public reports do not provide a breakdown of the job losses. It is therefore too early to quantify the effects on Dutch employees or Dutch productions.

The redundancies in themselves also do not indicate how the streaming service will adjust its strategy in the coming months. Some of the affected roles support the technology and operations behind the service, not necessarily the production of films and series itself. Further clarity must come from additional communication by Netflix and any financial documents.
