The British Film Institute is providing support through its Innovation Challenge Fund for eight independent creatives testing AI and other new technologies in real film projects. The programme combines training with legal, ethical and practical guidance.

On 11 November, the BFI announced that eight independent creatives would take part in a programme of structured training, mentoring and access to technical tools. The participants are working on their own creative projects and are expected to generate knowledge about the usefulness of new technology in development and production.

The scheme comes under the BFI National Lottery Innovation Challenge Fund. According to the BFI, the fund supports experiments intended to address persistent problems in the UK film sector and make the resulting experience available to a wider group of professionals. The BFI cites generative AI and virtual production as examples of technologies being explored in the sector.

According to the BFI, the selected projects will also receive legal guidance and attention to ethical frameworks. That guidance could shift the discussion from general demonstrations to concrete questions about rights, licensing, authorship, transparency and responsibility within a production process.

The practical component could be relevant to independent producers with limited access to specialist lawyers, test budgets or technical staff. A supported experiment can clarify which applications save time, which require additional oversight and which risks cannot be resolved through a simple workflow change.

The BFI does not publish separate amounts for each participant in the announcement. According to the application information, the Innovation Challenge Fund is part of a programme that aims to develop up to twelve challenge calls between 2026 and 2029. The application information states that previous calls distributed up to £1.8 million from the National Lottery allocation between 2024 and 2026.

The scheme is not a general grant for purchasing AI software. Its aim is to conduct research, provide guidance and gather useful insights for the sector. Nor does the announcement indicate that AI will replace human roles in the selected projects.

The main outcome will therefore not only be a finished product, but also documentation of what works within independent production. For European producers outside the UK, the programme is of interest as a practical example of sector policy on AI, but the results are not yet available.
