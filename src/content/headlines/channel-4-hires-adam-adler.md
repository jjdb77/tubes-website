Channel 4 has appointed Adam Adler as creative director of its in-house production company. The appointment forms part of its strategy to develop more original formats and intellectual property after the UK's Media Act 2024 eased previous restrictions on publisher-broadcasters.

Adler will take up the new role under Sarah Dillistone, who has led Channel 4's in-house production activities since February. He previously worked at Endemol, Granada, Objective and Potato and ran his own label, Gameface. At Channel 4, he was already a commissioning editor for entertainment.

As an executive producer and showrunner, Adler worked on The Cube, among other programmes. Channel 4 also cites the upcoming reality series Derren Brown: Incognito as part of his recent work. The broadcaster is now positioning him as responsible for the creative development of a new ideas studio.

The aim is broader than filling an in-house channel schedule. Channel 4 says it wants to develop formats that can be sold, licensed or produced internationally. The broadcaster says this is an attempt to create new revenue streams alongside advertising, sponsorship and distribution deals.

The move follows a change to UK regulation. The Media Act 2024 made it possible for Channel 4 to build more in-house production value and intellectual property. The broadcaster formally remains a publisher-broadcaster, but can therefore take a larger share in the development and exploitation of programme ideas.

Channel 4 says there will continue to be an operational separation between the new ideas studio and the commissioning department. Sarah Dillistone reports to chief executive Priya Dogra, while the in-house studio will have its own creative remit. That separation could help prevent Channel 4 from using its role as commissioner to give its own productions automatic priority.

For independent producers, the development could bring both competition and collaboration. A strong in-house studio could create additional demand for creators, executive producers and format developers. At the same time, Channel 4 may want to retain a larger share of the rights and international revenues from successful ideas. The operation's financial scale and its first concrete projects have not yet been disclosed.
