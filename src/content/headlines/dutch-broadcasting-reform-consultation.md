The online consultation on reforming the national public broadcasting system closes today. The proposal turns the current eleven broadcasting associations into four broadcasting houses and merges NOS and NTR into a single task organisation.

Today is the final day of the online consultation. Once the consultation has ended, the responses will be processed before the proposal is sent to the Council of State for advice. It will then be considered by the House of Representatives and the Senate. The Cabinet is aiming to introduce the new system on 1 January 2029.

The core of the plan is a fixed structure with four broadcasting houses, NOS and NPO as the coordinating organisation. Under the plans, the current membership system would disappear. As a result, broadcasters would no longer have a position based on membership numbers, and new associations could no longer join by recruiting members.

NOS and NTR would continue together under the name NOS. NTR is currently the task broadcaster for areas including culture, education and young audiences; NOS mainly provides news, sport and parliamentary coverage. The proposed structure could affect the allocation of commissions and multi-year agreements with producers.

NPO has also announced that it wants to end NPO Campus Radio, NPO Soul & Jazz, NPO 2 extra and BVN. A reorganisation of the NPO organisation is also in the pipeline. According to NPO, these decisions are linked to a structural cut in funding from 2027 and the desire to free up resources for digital platforms.

The proposal raises questions about the implications for culture, religion and philosophy, journalism and regional production. How will budgets, airtime and capacity for production commissions be divided among the new broadcasting houses?
