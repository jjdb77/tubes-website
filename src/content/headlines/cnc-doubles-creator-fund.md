France’s Centre national du cinéma et de l’image animée (CNC) will resume its fund for creation on social platforms in 2027. The annual budget will be €6 million, after the programme was previously suspended following criticism of the impartiality of the assessment procedure.

The CNC is linking the resumption to the publication on 5 November of a report by Maxime Boutron on support for creation on social platforms. According to the CNC, the fund is intended for makers of YouTube and Twitch projects, among others, but is also open to media companies submitting an eligible documentary or fiction project.

The new annual allocation is €6 million. According to AFP, published by TV5MONDE, that amount is double the sum allocated for 2026 before the fund was suspended. The CNC is therefore once again reserving a budget for creation on social platforms.

The scheme is not a general production subsidy for influencers. The CNC says projects must have artistic ambition and a clear directorial intention. Advertising videos and commercial segments within a video are excluded from support. Brand partnerships must not determine the editorial content.

Access for established media is also relevant. According to the CNC, media organisations can apply when they submit an eligible work and meet the specific conditions, such as requirements concerning reach or previous CNC support. This creates a scheme that can serve both individual makers and professional production companies.

The resumption follows a crisis of confidence. The fund was suspended after controversy over the impartiality of the committee and reports of threats against committee members. The way the selection procedure will be organised is therefore also at issue when it restarts.

For producers in other European countries, the institutional choice is particularly significant: a film fund is no longer treating platform video solely as marketing or short-form commercial content. The French approach could further blur the boundary between traditional audiovisual production and creator production, but according to sector representatives the relatively limited budget still falls short of needs.

The amount is a nominal subsidy allocation, not a measure of the creator sector’s overall size or profitability. The CNC’s annual budget alone does not show how many projects can be supported with it.
