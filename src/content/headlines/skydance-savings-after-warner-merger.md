Skydance announced its first major financial plans following the acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery on Monday. The group aims to save $6 billion over three years, while also bringing together the streaming operations of HBO Max and Paramount+.

The cuts are a direct consequence of the merger, which made Skydance one of the world’s largest entertainment and media companies. The group includes Warner Bros. and Paramount film studios, television network CBS and news channel CNN.

According to the company, at least $2.2 billion of the savings will come from overheads and property. Another $1 billion is to be found in marketing costs. Technology costs should fall by about $1.4 billion. Skydance expects to achieve 30 per cent of the total savings in the first year.

The scale of the plans is linked to its financial position after the acquisition. According to Reuters, Skydance has $87 billion in gross debt and about $10 billion in cash. The company says, according to Reuters, that it wants to become more efficient while continuing to invest in streaming, where it expects growth.

An initial product decision is the planned bundle of HBO Max and Paramount+. It is due to become available at the start of next year. The two services together reached 203 million subscribers worldwide, but Skydance has announced that it will no longer publish separate quarterly figures for subscriber numbers. The group wants to place greater emphasis on profitability and viewer engagement.

Paramount reported revenue growth of 5 per cent to $7 billion for the quarter that ended in September. Revenue at Paramount+ rose by 21 per cent. Those figures date from before the completion of the purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery and therefore say nothing yet about the performance of the fully merged company.

The integration also brings creative and organisational changes. James Gunn and Peter Safran stepped down as leaders of DC Studios and were replaced by Jeremy Latcham. Skydance also warned that disappointing Warner films could reduce fourth-quarter profit by up to $200 million.

For employees, creators and viewers, the merger mainly means uncertainty. The company has not yet announced how many jobs will be lost as a result of the savings. For consumers, the bundle may offer convenience, but no longer publishing subscriber numbers separately will make it harder to track how the services are developing.

Paramount’s revenue growth and the combined subscriber total are not measures of the new group’s profitability. The $6 billion in savings are a company target, not yet an achieved result.
