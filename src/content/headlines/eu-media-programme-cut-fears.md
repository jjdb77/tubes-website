More than fifty film festivals and audiovisual organisations are protesting against a proposed 30 per cent cut to AgoraEU, the future EU programme for culture, media and civic participation. Under the proposal, the budget would fall from approximately €8.6 billion to €6 billion for the 2028-2034 period.

The warning comes as member states negotiate the European Union’s next Multiannual Financial Framework. The Irish EU presidency’s text provides a basis for negotiations, not a final decision. The European Council will discuss the priorities, followed by further negotiations with the European Parliament and the European Commission.

From 2028, AgoraEU is intended to bring together several existing funding strands. These include the successor to Creative Europe MEDIA, support for culture, and programmes covering media, democracy and civic participation. For the audiovisual sector, the MEDIA strand is particularly relevant, as it supports European development, distribution, festivals, markets and cross-border cooperation.

In a joint appeal, sixteen film and audiovisual organisations warn that a smaller budget could weaken the position of independent producers and international cooperation. Signatories include UNIC, FIAPF, FERA, the Federation of Screenwriters in Europe, the European Producers Club and Europa International, among others.

The European Broadcasting Union has also joined the protest. The EBU reports that almost 600 organisations from the media, culture and civil society sectors oppose the proposed cut. According to the union, reliable media infrastructure is part of Europe’s democratic infrastructure.

Nothing is changing today for producers with regard to ongoing MEDIA schemes. The risk lies in the next programme period: fewer resources could lead to smaller calls, stricter selection or less room for development and distribution. The precise consequences can only be determined once the EU institutions reach agreement on the overall budget and the allocation within AgoraEU.
