Paramount has closed its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The combined group is called Skydance and is valued at around 111 billion dollars, including the debt it took on. Warner Bros. Discovery shares have left the Nasdaq and Skydance trades under a new ticker.

The new company owns two film studios, two streaming services and a large package of television and sports rights. Paramount Pictures, Warner Bros., CBS, CNN, HBO, Paramount+ and HBO Max now sit in one group, together with cable channels and large film and series libraries.

Paramount presents the deal as a way to gain scale at a time when streaming is expensive to make and traditional TV revenue is falling. It aims for at least 6 billion dollars a year in savings within three years. The takeover was largely paid for with debt.

Competition authorities in almost seventy jurisdictions approved it, and a US judge cleared a settlement with several states last week. For producers the practical questions are still open. Which teams merge, which titles go to cinemas or to streaming, and how the two services are bundled will be decided in the coming months.
