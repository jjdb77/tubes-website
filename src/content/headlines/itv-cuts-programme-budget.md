ITV is taking £25 million out of its programming budget in the fourth quarter. According to ITV’s trading update, the measure is part of total temporary savings of £30 million, after advertising revenues fell by 8 per cent in the third quarter.

ITV said in a trading update on Wednesday that advertising revenues in the fourth quarter are expected to be 5 to 10 per cent lower than a year earlier. For 2026 as a whole, the broadcaster expects a fall of 2 to 3 per cent. The company points to weaker demand and broader economic uncertainty.

According to ITV’s trading update, £25 million of the £30 million in temporary savings will come from content and £5 million from discretionary spending outside content. According to The Guardian, smaller external commissions in particular will be deferred until 2027. ITV says the measure will not lead to staff reductions.

The total programming budget for 2026 is approximately £1.2 billion. The measure therefore affects a limited part of annual spending, but deferring commissions could have consequences for independent producers.

ITV’s studio and distribution arm is excluded from the sale of the television and streaming operations to Sky. Studio revenue fell by 1 per cent in the first nine months. At the same time, five of the ten most-watched British dramas in the third quarter came from productions involving ITV Studios, including The Gentlemen for Netflix and The Blame for ITV.

ITV expects lower advertising revenues for its broadcasting operations, while the studio arm continues to deliver production work. The announced sale of the television and streaming operations to Sky is still being examined by Ofcom and the UK competition authority following intervention by Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy.

The saving is temporary, but the source indicates that smaller external productions could be shifted mainly to 2027. The £1.2 billion figure refers to the total programming budget and does not indicate which genres or producers will be affected.
