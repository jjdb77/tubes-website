UK film and high-end television production reached £4.1 billion in the first nine months of 2026. That is lower than in the same period of 2025, when the BFI recorded £5.5 billion.

The British Film Institute published new official statistics on film and high-end television production on Tuesday. Combined spending from January to September 2026 was 25.5 per cent lower than a year earlier. In 2024, the comparable total was £4.7 billion.

The decline is visible in both film and high-end television. Film production reached approximately £1.3 billion, according to reporting on the BFI figures, compared with £2 billion in the first nine months of 2025. High-end television accounted for 133 programmes, with £2.79 billion in UK spending.

The figures cover productions that meet the UK’s cultural and tax requirements. They therefore do not measure all filming taking place in the United Kingdom, nor do they provide a complete picture of post-production, development or spending outside the country.

The decline comes as UK producers face more cautious commissioning by broadcasters and streamers. The Guardian linked the figures to announced savings at ITV, which is removing £25 million from its fourth-quarter content budget. That is a separate corporate decision and is not part of the BFI statistic.

For the sector, the combination of lower domestic commissions and fewer large international productions is particularly relevant. The figures do not show that the UK production system is becoming structurally smaller: the BFI published a nine-month snapshot, while tax regimes, international co-productions and projects starting later may still affect the full-year result.

The figures cover spending on film and high-end television production over nine months. They do not provide a full-year picture and do not cover the entire audiovisual sector in the UK.
