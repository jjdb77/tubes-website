Ofcom has tightened its guidance on the welfare of participants in so-called constructed-reality programmes. The UK regulator says the emphasis is on formats in which participants share personal experiences or engage in emotional or physical intimacy.

The new guidance falls under Section Seven of the Broadcasting Code, the rules on fairness and the treatment of participants. Ofcom says it does not approve or ban formats and does not want to restrict broadcasters’ editorial freedom. According to Ofcom, producers and broadcasters must put appropriate safeguards in place.

For higher-risk productions, Ofcom lists possible measures including a clearly identifiable welfare team independent of the editorial department, access to external welfare specialists and specialist support services. Ofcom also says that contact with family and friends away from the set during production is important.

The obligations apply not only during filming. Ofcom emphasises that risks must be assessed before, during and after production. This can have implications for the entire production chain, from casting and the provision of information to post-broadcast support.

For producers, the tightening may prompt them to set out risk assessments and responsibilities earlier in the production plan. Commissioners may also seek greater clarity during tenders and format development over who is responsible for support and when external assistance will be brought in.

The measure comes in a genre that Ofcom says is popular with audiences and important to the UK production industry. Future regulatory decisions and complaints procedures may provide greater clarity on how it is applied.
