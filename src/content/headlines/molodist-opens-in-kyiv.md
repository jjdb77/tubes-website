The 55th edition of the international Molodist film festival opened in Kyiv on Saturday. The programme includes 120 films from 50 countries, including Ukrainian and international premieres.

The opening ceremony took place at Kyiv’s National Academic Operetta Theatre. The festival runs from 24 October to 1 November inclusive and uses venues including the Zhovten cinema and the House of Cinema.

The organisation is presenting a programme of debut films, student films, documentaries, feature films and experimental productions. It also includes films previously shown at festivals in Cannes, Berlin, Venice and Locarno.

The opening film is the Ukrainian tragicomedy ‘Zhytye’ by Jevhen Matvijenko. According to reports, the film consists of six stories about the daily lives of Ukrainians during the full-scale war.

Molodist is regarded as an important platform for young filmmakers and international debuts. The competition includes student films, short films and first features; there are also national and non-competitive programmes.

The festival is taking place in Kyiv while Ukraine continues to be affected by the war. The organisation has therefore also introduced a special rate for military personnel; the precise security measures surrounding individual screenings are not described in the sources consulted.

For Dutch readers, the opening is particularly relevant as a signal of the position of Ukraine’s film sector. Despite the war, Kyiv remains an international meeting place for filmmakers, jury members and audiences. The sources give no indication that Dutch productions or institutions are playing a particularly prominent role this year.
