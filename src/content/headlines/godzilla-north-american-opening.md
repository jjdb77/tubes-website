Godzilla Minus Zero took $31.6 million in North America during its opening weekend. According to AP, that is the biggest debut for a live-action film in a language other than English, excluding The Passion of the Christ.

The film was released in North American cinemas on the same day as in Japan. AP reports that Godzilla Minus Zero took approximately $17 million in Japan over the weekend. According to AP, GKids is the US distributor and the film is produced by Japan's Toho.

With $31.6 million, the film surpassed the previous live-action record held by Hero, which took $17.9 million in 2004. The figure is also above the $20.5 million with which Warner Bros. opened The Cat in the Hat, according to studio estimates.

The comparison does not apply to all foreign productions. AP cites the anime Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle, which opened to $70.6 million last year, as a higher debut for a non-English-language production. It is not a live-action film and therefore falls outside the specific record.

For Toho, the simultaneous international release is an important part of its strategy. Koji Ueda, president of Toho Global, said according to AP that the film was released simultaneously in its home market and the United States in order to serve the global audience directly.

The result is relevant to European distributors acquiring Japanese franchise films. It does not prove that a comparable result will follow in Western Europe, but it does reduce the commercial risk that distributors associate with a non-English-language live-action title supported by a major international campaign.

The figures are preliminary weekend estimates. They say something about the opening, but not yet about the eventual box-office takings, European performance or the split between producer, distributors and cinemas.

The opening primarily measures initial demand for the franchise. Without European figures, marketing costs and the later cinema run, no conclusion can yet be drawn about ultimate profitability.
