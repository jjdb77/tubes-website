A California Court of Appeal has rejected Warner Bros. Television’s attempt to block a legal case over the series The Pitt and ER. The case brought by Sherri Crichton, the widow of ER creator Michael Crichton, can therefore proceed with disclosure and a jury trial.

The three judges unanimously ruled that there are sufficient indications of a case involving possible derivative rights. According to the court, the medical dramas display clear similarities, including an emergency department, overlapping crises, medical jargon and exhausted doctors and nurses.

The ruling does not mean that The Pitt has been legally classified as an unauthorised reboot of ER. The court decided only that the case may not be dismissed at this stage. The final assessment of the contractual and copyright claims is still to come.

Crichton’s estate says that The Pitt arose from failed discussions about a new version of ER. According to The Guardian, a 1994 contract contained what is known as a frozen-rights provision, meaning that spin-offs, remakes and other derivative works could be made only with the consent of the rights holders involved.

Warner Bros. Television and the producers argued that the similarities mainly involved general features of hospital dramas. In their view, it cannot be said that every series about an emergency department is automatically a derivative work of ER. The court found the similarities cited provisionally strong enough for the proceedings to continue.

The appeal court kept Warner Bros. Television as a defendant in the case. Actor Noah Wyle, producer John Wells and showrunner R. Scott Gemmill were reportedly removed from the case because it had not been sufficiently shown that they knew their work could infringe contractual rights.

The case could end in a settlement or a jury trial in Los Angeles. For the television industry, the dispute concerns the boundary between recognisable genre features and a protected sequel or derivative work. The ruling does not yet settle the eventual legal consequences for The Pitt.
