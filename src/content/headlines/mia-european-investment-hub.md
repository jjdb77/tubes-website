The twelfth edition of the MIA market in Rome has opened with the launch of the European Investment Hub. The new programme brings producers, investors, public institutions and financial parties together around film and television financing.

The European Investment Hub, or EIH for short, has been established as a platform for discussions on financial instruments for the audiovisual sector. It was launched during the opening of MIA, the Mercato Internazionale Audiovisivo, which is being held in Rome from 19 to 23 October.

The programme focuses on how European productions and production companies can attract more private capital alongside traditional subsidies. Representatives of European institutions, national ministries, regional film funds and the financial sector attended the opening session.

According to MIA, the aim is not only to provide additional financing for individual projects, but also to improve access to capital for European businesses. This affects producers seeking to scale up, retain ownership of rights for longer or develop co-productions with international partners.

The EIH offers meetings with parties involved in media finance, lending, completion finance and investment. The programme features six financing opportunities, including CAA Media Finance, Media Capital Technologies, Coficiné, Head Gear Films, Lief Entertainment and Cofiloisirs.

The focus on private finance comes at a time when the European sector is facing rising production costs and sharper international competition. The opening also addressed the relatively small scale of many European companies and the need to connect policymakers, financiers and producers more systematically.

One political issue is the future EU budget. According to MIA, Giuseppe Abbamonte, director of media policy at the European Commission, pointed to the EIH’s role in attracting private capital. Cineuropa also reported that he criticised a proposal to cut the future AgoraEU budget, which would include Creative Europe MEDIA, by around 30 per cent, from €8.6 billion to €6 billion.

The financing hub does not directly alter existing national funds or European subsidy schemes. The new meeting structure could offer producers an additional route to investors. If investments are made, rights, territories and conditions attached to public support may play a role.

For Western European producers, the combination of public support and private money could create new financing opportunities. The viability of the EIH will depend on whether discussions result in concrete investments, and whether projects can become financeable without placing excessive pressure on creative control or public-interest objectives.
