The French Assemblée nationale is considering amendments this week to the 2027 budget that could affect the funding of film, television and online video. One proposal seeks to scrap the tax discount for free video platforms, while public-service broadcasting remains under budgetary pressure.

The debate forms part of the Projet de loi de finances 2027, France’s annual budget bill. According to Legiwatch, 249 amendments affecting audiovisual media, film or culture had been registered by 17 October. Consideration by the Finance Committee continues on Monday.

One proposal seeks to scrap the 66 per cent discount that free video platforms receive on the French taxe sur les services de vidéo. According to the explanatory notes to the amendment, the tax benefits the Centre national du cinéma et de l’image animée (CNC), the French institution that supports film and audiovisual production.

The Assemblée nationale published the amendment as a proposal by MPs who believe the original discount no longer reflects the current market. According to its sponsors, the scheme was introduced when the services concerned mainly distributed user-generated videos. They argue that platforms now also offer professional productions and compete directly with other audiovisual services.

The proposal is not yet law. It may be amended, rejected or made part of a compromise during the parliamentary process. For platforms and producers, the key issue is whether the tax base and the contribution to the CNC will change structurally.

Public-service broadcasting is also central to the debate. According to CB News, the 2027 budget proposal provides €3.82 billion in public funding for the public audiovisual sector. It is not yet clear whether France Télévisions would receive €47 million less than in the original 2026 budget. The parliamentary process still has to determine the final funding.

France’s budget therefore determines not only the scope available to France Télévisions, Radio France and France Médias Monde, but also affects the wider funding chain in which the CNC, broadcasters, producers and distributors work together. The forthcoming votes are therefore relevant to both public commissioning bodies and commercial video services.
