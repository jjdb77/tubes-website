Three Dutch organisations for writers and journalists, the copyright society Lira, the authors' union Auteursbond and the journalists' union NVJ, held a protest outside Facebook's Amsterdam office on 29 September. They are also gathering support for collective proceedings against Meta.

The organisations say texts by freelance writers, journalists and translators were used to train language models such as Meta's Llama, without permission or payment. They earlier asked Meta formally to stop, and say that did not lead to talks. No court has ruled on the use of the texts.

Their demands come down to three things. Creators should be paid when their work is used, have a say in the conditions, and be able to see which sources an AI model was trained on. Creators can sign up through the Opstand campaign.

For film and television this touches scripts, treatments and subtitles as much as journalism. Whatever comes out of the proceedings is likely to shape how permission, exceptions in copyright law and collective licences work for AI training in the Netherlands.
