UK Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy issued a Public Interest Intervention Notice on 4 November concerning Sky UK’s proposed acquisition of ITV M&E Holdings. The transaction will therefore be examined for possible implications for the public interest before a formal competition investigation can begin.

The notice was published by the UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport. The document names Sky UK as the buyer and ITV M&E Holdings as the target. The procedure does not mean that the acquisition has been blocked or that a final judgement has already been reached.

The move gives the UK government scope to have media interests assessed alongside competition issues. In the United Kingdom, the minister can issue a public-interest intervention notice for this purpose. The Competition and Markets Authority can then be asked to investigate relevant aspects of the transaction.

According to its own information, the CMA has not yet opened a formal investigation into this transaction. The acquisition is therefore at a preliminary regulatory stage. For Sky and ITV, this may create uncertainty over timing, conditions and final approval.

The commercial stakes are high because ITV M&E Holdings covers part of ITV’s media activities. The assessment therefore concerns not only distribution and advertising markets, but also the position of a major UK commercial broadcaster in production, news, rights and digital services.

The procedure could become relevant for producers and independent makers if regulators impose requirements concerning access to content, news provision, advertising data or the treatment of independent production. It is not yet known what conditions, if any, may follow.

The UK government has not published a decision stating that the acquisition is contrary to the public interest. The current development is solely the start of the intervention procedure; a formal CMA phase and a final decision are still to come.
