The European Film Academy has announced the shortlist for the 39th European Film Awards. A total of 68 feature films have been selected for the nomination phase, which continues on 14 November with the announcement of most of the nominations.

The shortlist comprises 45 fiction films, 15 documentaries and eight animated films. The works represent 40 European countries and include both established creators and new filmmakers, as well as cross-border productions. According to the Academy, 25 of the selected films were directed or co-directed by women.

The European Film Academy is a membership organisation for professionals from the European film sector. Its members vote on the nominations and winners in most categories. The shortlist is therefore neither a jury prize nor a final nomination, but the first public selection on which the further campaign and voting are based.

For producers and distributors, the announcement has direct practical value. Under the Academy’s campaign rules, rights holders may use their selection from the shortlist publicly for promotional purposes. The Academy is also linking it to a European screening programme through partner cinemas in 125 cities.

That makes the shortlist relevant to sales discussions, festival positioning and cinema planning. A selected film gains additional visibility before the nominations are finalised. At the same time, the commercial effects remain uncertain: the selection guarantees neither a nomination, a distribution deal, a cinema release nor audience reach.

The nominations in most of the main categories will be announced on 14 November during the Thessaloniki International Film Festival. These include European Film, European Director, European Actress, European Actor, European Screenwriter, cinematography, editing, production design and sound.

The awards ceremony is scheduled for 16 January 2027 in Athens. For European sales agents and distributors, the key new information now lies in the combination of visibility and timing: until early November, selected films can build on their status, after which the nomination phase may further alter their market value.
