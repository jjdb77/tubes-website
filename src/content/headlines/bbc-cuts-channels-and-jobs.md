The BBC will stop three linear services in 2027 and also plans to cut more than 100 positions at BBC News. The measures are part of a broader savings plan through which the British public broadcaster says it wants to save hundreds of millions of pounds.

The three services being discontinued as linear broadcasts are BBC Three, BBC Scotland and BBC Radio 4 Extra. The BBC wants to continue parts of the offering online, including via iPlayer. The channels will therefore not disappear entirely as brands or programme offerings, but the way audiences receive them will change.

According to reports, more than 100 positions will disappear at BBC News. This is a proposal that has been communicated internally to staff. The measure comes alongside earlier plans to cut up to around 2,000 jobs in total across the broadcaster in the coming years.

The BBC links the move to a savings target of around £500 million. The broadcaster is under pressure from rising costs, changing viewing and listening habits, and uncertainty over income from the British licence fee. The precise consequences for each department may still change during consultations with staff and trade unions.

The choice of online distribution fits into a broader trend among public broadcasters. Younger viewers are increasingly using streaming platforms and apps, while linear channels have relatively high fixed costs. At the same time, the BBC remains responsible for a broad audience, including people with less digital access.

For Dutch viewers, the decision is particularly relevant because the BBC is an important international news and programme producer. Reduced capacity at BBC News could affect international reporting and cooperation. Whether the move online will genuinely maintain reach depends on access to iPlayer, usage among different age groups and the quality of the digital offering.

The announced closures do not yet amount to the complete abolition of the programme strands involved. The BBC has indicated that the services will remain available online, while the precise timetable and staffing consequences are still being worked out.
