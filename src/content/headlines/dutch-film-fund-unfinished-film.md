The Netherlands Film Fund pledged more than €600,000 for a feature film that, according to Nieuwsuur, was not released. Nieuwsuur’s investigation found that there were doubts about the financing at an early stage and that the fund did not sufficiently check whether the project was completed.

The film in question is Lucca Mortis, directed by Peter Greenaway. According to Nieuwsuur, he is a British director who lives in Amsterdam. Dustin Hoffman and Helen Hunt were attached to the project. The expected budget was more than €13 million, according to Nieuwsuur.

The subsidy was pledged in 2023 under a scheme intended to encourage Dutch filmmakers and film producers to work in the Netherlands. According to the reports, the Film Fund made exceptions to its own rules. The fund also reportedly failed to check whether production was ultimately completed.

A subsidy for a film is no guarantee that the finished product will reach cinemas. Film projects can collapse because of financing problems, changes in production or the withdrawal of partners. According to the reports, the main question in this case is whether the fund had sufficient assurance beforehand that the project was viable and whether it exercised adequate oversight afterwards.

The case therefore touches on how public film funding is distributed. The Film Fund uses subsidies to make productions possible that would struggle to get off the ground without public support. On the other hand, taxpayers’ money is spent efficiently only when criteria are applied consistently and risks are accounted for transparently.

The precise financial settlement of the production is not fully public in the sources available for this article. Nor has it been established that fraud or criminal offences were involved. The reports concern the fund’s methods and oversight, not a court ruling.

For the Dutch film sector, the matter could affect future subsidy rules. Stricter conditions could better protect public funds, but also make risky international productions more difficult. The Film Fund will have to explain why the rules were departed from in this case and what oversight now applies.
