Netflix says it has produced or co-produced more than two hundred Korean titles in ten years. According to figures from the streaming service, more than 95,000 actors, creators, technicians and temporary workers in South Korea were involved.

Netflix announced the figures on Friday, marking the tenth anniversary of its South Korean service. Since its launch in 2016, the company says it has worked with more than eighty local production companies.

According to Yonhap, the 95,000 people mentioned include more than 25,000 cast and crew members and more than 70,000 extras and workers hired on a daily basis. The figures come from a special website Netflix published for the anniversary.

According to Netflix, the productions were filmed at more than one hundred locations in South Korea. Netflix says more than two hundred Korean productions have appeared in the service’s global top ten since 2021.

Netflix says that around 80 per cent of its global subscribers have watched Korean content at some point. That figure concerns viewing experiences and does not mean that 80 per cent of subscribers regularly watch Korean productions.

The streaming service also points to investments in training. Between 2021 and 2025, more than 6,500 people are said to have taken part in programmes for production development and training. The figures are company data and do not provide a fully independent picture of the entire South Korean audiovisual sector.
