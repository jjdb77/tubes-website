The Centre national du cinéma et de l’image animée (CNC) is making €1.5 million available for additional content for existing video games. French studios can submit projects for expansions and ongoing live-ops activities until 14 December.

The scheme targets games whose first commercial release took place no more than 36 months ago and which have sold more than 100,000 copies. The CNC is therefore choosing projects with demonstrable commercial success, rather than new games in the concept or pre-production phase.

The scheme covers downloadable content, usually abbreviated as DLC, and live ops. The latter includes temporary events, seasonal content and new gameplay experiences developed after the original release. The call is aimed at additional content for already released games.

The subsidy comes on top of the €5 million annual budget of the Fonds d’aide au jeu vidéo. It is unclear whether that fund has been managed and financed exclusively by the CNC since 2021. This call is specifically aimed at additional content after commercial launch.

Applicants must submit their applications by 2pm on 14 December 2026 at the latest. One of the conditions is that the game must have sold more than 100,000 copies. Applicants can consult the official call for the application requirements and assessment criteria.

The CNC links the measure to the economic pressure on France’s games industry. According to the institution, work on existing titles can provide teams with work for longer, retain player communities and create additional revenue. This is a policy justification for the scheme; its eventual impact on employment or turnover cannot yet be established.

For studios, the scheme may help finance further development after launch. DLC and live ops can give existing players new reasons to continue playing a game. The scheme could therefore extend the economic lifespan of French IP, although support remains dependent on selection and available funding.

The call was announced on 23 October during Paris Games Week. Applicants can consult the CNC’s official call for the implementation arrangements and any funding limits per project.
