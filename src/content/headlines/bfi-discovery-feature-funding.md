The British Film Institute opens a new round of its Discovery Feature Funding on Thursday. Debut directors can apply for funding for feature films with production budgets of between £1 million and £3.5 million.

According to the BFI, the scheme is part of the BFI National Lottery Filmmaking Fund. The fund’s publication says the round opens on 12 November 2026. Up to £1 million is available per project for development and production.

The scheme is intended for projects with a debut director. The fund is therefore targeting filmmakers who have not yet delivered a feature film, but can present a project with sufficient maturity for production and financing potential.

The BFI links the funding to a production budget of between £1 million and £3.5 million. The scheme therefore falls within the independent feature film segment, which is often too large for private financing alone and too small for the largest international studio investments.

Applicants must follow the terms of the full scheme. The public overview page gives the maximum amount and budget limits, but its summary does not provide a complete overview of the selection criteria, decision deadlines or number of projects available.

For producers, the timing is particularly relevant. The application round opens on the same day as the publication of this edition; the funding can serve as part of a broader mix with producer contributions, pre-sales, co-productions and other public funds. The BFI overview page does not state how many projects will be supported in this round.

The scheme does not guarantee that a project will ultimately be produced. The funding can reduce the financing gap for new directors and help them make a feature film.
