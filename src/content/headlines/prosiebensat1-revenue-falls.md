ProSiebenSat.1 reported revenue of €2.244 billion for the first nine months of 2026, 11 per cent less than a year earlier. The group confirmed its profit outlook but expects a moderate decline in group revenue for the full year 2026.

The figures are based on preliminary results published by the German media group on 12 November. ProSiebenSat.1 attributes the revenue decline to a challenging market environment and divestments. The company also cites the downturn in the television advertising market as a factor in its outlook.

At the same time, the group said earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation increased by €159 million. ProSiebenSat.1 also confirmed its expectation that profitability in 2026 will be clearly above the 2025 level. According to the company, EBITDA for 2025 was €241 million.

The figures therefore show a classic shift: lower revenue is accompanied by a higher profit measure. This may point to cost control, a different mix of activities or the effect of divested units. The preliminary statement does not indicate that underlying demand for all television and streaming activities has improved.

Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was €2.244 billion. According to the company, group revenue in 2025 was €3.675 billion. These amounts cover different periods and are not directly comparable. The company says that divestments also contributed to the decline in revenue compared with the same period a year earlier.

ProSiebenSat.1 expects a moderate decline in group revenue for the full year 2026. The group is maintaining its other key financial targets. According to the company, the final figures for the third quarter and the first nine months will be published later as a full report; the figures used now are preliminary results.

For producers and broadcasters in Germany, developments in the advertising market may be relevant. Lower revenue could put pressure on programming budgets, format acquisitions and producers’ negotiating position. The improvement in profit could at the same time provide room to continue investments or debt reduction, but the announcement does not specify how much money is going to content.

The main caveat is that revenue and profit do not measure the same thing. The preliminary figures show improved profitability on a smaller revenue base, but in themselves say little about audience reach, advertising prices, streaming subscribers or the allocation of resources across individual channels.

The revenue decline is not a complete measure of the underlying advertising market, because ProSiebenSat.1 also cites divestments. The figures are preliminary and do not show how revenue and profit are distributed across individual channels, streaming services or production budgets.
