Netflix plans to cut around 6 per cent of its worldwide workforce, almost 1,000 jobs. The streaming service cites organisational changes while saying it will continue investing in areas intended to strengthen growth and service.

The move was announced on Wednesday 14 October in an internal memo from co-chief executives Greg Peters and Ted Sarandos. According to The Associated Press, it is Netflix’s largest round of redundancies in years. Netflix has not published separate figures for Europe, country organisations or production teams.

Most roles are expected to disappear in product, technology, legal and human resources, according to available reports. The measure therefore mainly affects support and platform-focused activities. Netflix has provided no information on the impact on commissioning, local productions or production volumes in the Netherlands, the UK, Germany, France and other European markets.

Peters and Sarandos write that the company is strong and growing, but needs to make sharper choices about where staff and resources are deployed. The wording points to a redistribution of capacity, not automatically to a general production stoppage. That distinction is relevant for producers who might otherwise translate the redundancies directly into fewer commissions.

The timing coincides with Netflix’s further expansion beyond the traditional subscription model. The service is investing in advertising, live programming and games, among other areas. In Spain, Netflix also secured exclusive rights to broadcast the 2030 FIFA World Cup, making live content a larger part of its strategy.

For European partners, the distribution of the cuts is particularly important. A smaller central technology or legal organisation could affect platform development, subtitling, rights administration and the speed at which local productions are launched. There is currently no regional breakdown to clarify this. Nor is it known whether roles will be replaced by outsourcing or automation.

The announced reduction therefore says mainly something about Netflix’s internal cost and organisational structure. In itself, it is not evidence that the streamer is reducing its European investment obligations or local content budgets. Producers and facilities companies will have to await the coming quarterly updates and any regional staff announcements.
