The European Broadcasting Union is expanding its sovereign AI framework together with NVIDIA. According to NVIDIA, the initiative should help public media organisations use AI agents without surrendering control of data, infrastructure, models and security.

The expansion was reported on Thursday in connection with the cooperation between the EBU and NVIDIA. According to NVIDIA, the framework is intended for public broadcasters using AI in newsrooms, production, archives and audience services. New components include sovereign cloud providers, developers of open models and systems integrators.

According to the explanation, the framework should produce practical requirements and reference architectures. These describe where AI processing takes place, how data is managed, which models can be used and how systems are secured throughout their entire lifecycle. This could shift the debate from general AI principles towards technical procurement and implementation choices.

For European media companies, the location of processing may be relevant. Archive material, unpublished productions and editorial files may be subject to contractual, copyright or privacy restrictions. Locally or European-managed infrastructure can reduce dependence on a single US cloud or model provider, but does not automatically resolve those legal and operational questions.

According to reports, Microsoft is providing expertise around Azure Local and Foundry Local. Nebius is mentioned as a platform for training, fine-tuning and running models on European infrastructure. Qvest and other integrators are expected to translate the reference architectures into systems that can be introduced into existing broadcasting environments.

Alongside the EBU framework, a separate improvement to Adobe Frame.io was reported. With NVIDIA acceleration, production teams can search video libraries semantically; according to Adobe, this involves embedding generation that is approximately five times faster, processing capacity up to four times higher and search latency up to five times lower. NVIDIA names German producer UFA as a user.

The EBU has more than 115 member organisations in more than 55 countries. NVIDIA describes the new framework as a collection of requirements and architectural choices on which broadcasters can base their own tenders and AI pilot projects. Its practical value may also depend on the extent to which suppliers offer interoperable systems and verifiable security measures.
