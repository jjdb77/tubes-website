The European Broadcasting Union is asking Meta to amend its new commercial terms. According to the union, Meta and its AI agents can scrape content from linked websites through business accounts, while users can currently refuse only by leaving the platform.

The EBU represents public service broadcasters that use Meta services for pages, channels and audience reach. According to the union, the new terms could link consent to holding a Meta business account, including when the content originates from linked websites, archives or third parties.

The organisation wants access to Meta and consent for reuse to be legally separated. According to the EBU, an account should grant consent only for material that the account holder actually owns and for which permission has been given.

The union is also calling for an opt-out without adverse consequences. A broadcaster should be able to refuse the use of material for AI training or development without reduced reach, poorer distribution or the loss of referrals to its own website.

The EBU cites robots.txt and other machine-readable reservations as possible technical means of communicating restrictions. The union stresses that such signals will work only if Meta treats them as binding, both contractually and technically.

According to the EBU, the European Commission and the UK’s Competition and Markets Authority are examining the issue. The union says Meta has postponed the introduction of the terms, but that postponement does not resolve the underlying problem. The EBU reports no final decision by regulators.
