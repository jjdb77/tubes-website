Planning in Tubes is not a separate schedule that someone types over from the budget. It is the budget, laid out in time.

![Weekly planning calendar with activities](/assets/images/tubes-agenda.png)

## From budget to planning

Every budget header is an activity. When you choose **Plan this budget** on a budget, its activities become the base planning of the production. In the planning you give them their days, people, items and locations.

- An activity has a **window** on the timeline: the period in which its days can fall. Budget headers without dates wait in **Not on the timeline yet** until you set their days, or plan a day straight from them.
- A **day** is a planned day of work. A day draws from the envelope of every activity it serves; an activity can also spread over several days and call sheets.
- Every person, location or item you plan is a **budget line**. The strip at the top shows budget and planned for the activity, and moves as you plan. Red means over budget or double booked.

## Open the planning

**Planning** in the sidebar shows all productions at once; choose one to plan against its budget. Within a project, the **Production** tab has **Planning**, **Day Planning** and **Scheduler**.

The planning screen has tabs for the **Calendar**, the **Activities**, a single **Activity** and the **Resources** (people, locations, items). In advanced mode there are more lenses: the timeline, a board, crew swimlanes, workload, capacity, costs and the breakdown. See [Simple or advanced mode](/help/planning/simple-or-advanced-mode/).

## What a day costs

Rates come from the budget lines of the activities you tick. After saving a day you see what is left in their envelopes. The **Costs** tab adds it up: expected is planned days times the rates on the budget lines; spent is what is booked on the cost types of those lines. People and locations carry no rate; the item (a day rate, a studio day) is the bridge between planning and money.

> **Tip:** Name budget headers the way you would plan them ("Shoot week 1", "Edit"). Coarse headers give coarse activities; specific headers give a planning you can send call sheets from.
