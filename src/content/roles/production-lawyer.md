A producer has found the money for a film, but the bank will only release it once the rights in the novel, the director's deal and the sales agreement are all signed and match each other. Three weeks of drafts and calls later, everything closes on the same afternoon. The production lawyer is the person who writes and negotiates those contracts, so the project can legally be made and sold.

## What a production lawyer does

The production lawyer is usually an outside lawyer or a small law firm hired by the [producer](/production-roles/producer/). The work starts in [development](/how-a-film-is-made/#development), when the producer options a book or buys a script (an option is the exclusive right to develop a work for a set period). It grows heavier during [financing](/how-a-film-is-made/#financing), when many parties invest and each wants its own agreement.

In pre-production the lawyer drafts or checks the deals for the director, lead cast and key crew, and the agreements for locations, music and other material used on screen. After the shoot they help prepare the delivery paperwork that buyers and distributors require, including the chain of title. That is the set of documents showing the production really owns what it is selling.

## Responsibilities

- Secure and document the rights in the script, book or other source material
- Draft and negotiate financing agreements with investors, funds and banks
- Prepare and check cast, director and key crew contracts
- Advise on co-production agreements between companies in different countries
- Review clearances for music, archive footage, brands and locations
- Work with the [completion guarantor](/production-roles/completion-guarantor/) and the bank on the closing documents
- Assemble the chain of title and legal delivery items for the [sales agent](/production-roles/sales-agent/) and distributors

## How the role touches the budget

Through a legal fees line in the budget, usually a fixed fee or an agreed cap for the whole production. The bigger influence is indirect. The contracts the lawyer negotiates set fees, payment dates, overtime terms and deferments (payments postponed until the film earns money), and those numbers end up in the budget and the [cost report](/insights/what-is-a-production-cost-report/). A clause left vague can cost far more later than the legal fee itself.

## How people become production lawyers

They train and qualify as lawyers first, then specialise in media, entertainment or intellectual property law. Many spend years at a firm with a media practice or in the legal team of a broadcaster or studio before working directly with producers. Knowing how productions are financed and made matters as much as knowing the law.

## At a production company or broadcaster

Larger production companies and broadcasters have their own legal and business affairs department. There a [business affairs manager](/production-roles/business-affairs-manager/) negotiates the commercial terms, and an in-house lawyer or an outside firm turns them into contracts.
