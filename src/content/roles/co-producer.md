A drama is set partly on a northern coast, and the lead producer has most of the money at home. A co-producer abroad applies to a regional fund there, finds a local crew and books a sound studio for the mix. In return they share the rights to the film. The co-producer is a partner who brings money and work to a production in exchange for part of the ownership.

## What a co-producer does

In Europe the title usually means an international co-production. Two or more production companies, often in different countries, make one film together. The majority co-producer leads and puts in the largest share. The minority co-producer adds a smaller share, usually raised from funds, broadcasters and [incentives](/compare-film-incentives/) in their own country. Those sources often require part of the money to be spent locally, so the co-producer also runs that part of the work. That could be a few weeks of shooting, the visual effects or the sound mix.

The co-producer joins during [financing](/how-a-film-is-made/#financing), signs a co-production agreement with the lead [producer](/production-roles/producer/) and stays involved until delivery. In the United States the credit is used more loosely, sometimes for a senior member of the producing team rather than a partner company.

## Responsibilities

- Raise finance in their own territory, from funds, broadcasters and incentives
- Negotiate the co-production agreement, including rights and revenue shares
- Hire local crew, studios, post houses or [locations](/compare-film-tv-locations/)
- Make sure the required local spend is reached and documented
- Report costs to the lead producer and to their own funders
- Handle the local release or broadcast, where agreed

## How the role touches the budget

Directly, but for their own share. The co-producer manages the part of the budget spent in their country and has to prove it was spent there. That means separate cost reporting, local accounts and audits for the funds involved. The [production accountant](/production-roles/production-accountant/) often keeps the spend per country apart for this reason. If the local spend falls short, part of the funding can be at risk, so the co-producer watches that line closely.

## How people become co-producers

A co-producer is usually a producer with their own company. They take on a minority share of someone else's project to build relationships and a track record, while leading projects of their own at home. Film markets and co-production forums at [festivals](/compare-film-festivals/) are where many of these partnerships begin.

## In TV

In television, co-production often means two broadcasters or platforms in different countries sharing the cost of a series. Each takes the rights in its own territory. The production company leading the series then reports to both.
