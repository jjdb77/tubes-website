The producer likes the second draft, but the financier thinks the middle sags and the director wants the ending set at sea. The screenwriter takes the notes home, cuts a subplot, merges two characters and moves the last scene to a ferry. Four weeks later the third draft goes out. The screenwriter is the person who writes the story the whole production will be built on.

## What a screenwriter does

A screenwriter writes scripts for film and television. Some write their own original ideas and try to sell them. Others are hired by a [producer](/production-roles/producer/) to adapt a book, develop an idea or rewrite an existing script. Either way, the work usually starts in [development](/how-a-film-is-made/#development), long before there is any money for the film.

The process runs in stages. A short outline or treatment describes the story in prose. Then come the drafts, each followed by notes from the producer, the [script editor](/production-roles/script-editor/), the [director](/production-roles/director/) and often the financiers. A film can go through many drafts before it is ready to shoot. During production the writer may still do small rewrites, for example when a location falls through or an actor is replaced.

## Responsibilities

- Develop ideas into outlines, treatments and step outlines
- Write and rewrite drafts of the screenplay
- Work through notes from the producer, director and financiers
- Keep the story within the scale of the budget the producer has in mind
- Deliver scripts in the standard format, so they can be broken down and scheduled
- Do revisions in pre-production and during the shoot when needed

## How the role touches the budget

The screenwriter does not manage money, but every page they write becomes a cost. A night scene, a car chase, a period setting or a crowd all add days and departments. The [line producer](/production-roles/line-producer/) breaks the script down into scenes, cast, locations and days, and that breakdown is the start of the budget. The writer's own fee sits [above the line](/insights/above-the-line-vs-below-the-line/) and is often paid in steps, per draft or per stage of development.

## How people become screenwriters

There is no fixed route. Many writers start with short films, plays or spec scripts, which are scripts written without a commission to show what they can do. Others come from journalism, novels or comedy. In television, new writers often begin on long-running series or in a writers' room, and learn by writing episodes for someone else's show.

## In TV and commercials

In television many writers work together in a writers' room under a [showrunner](/production-roles/showrunner/), and each writer takes one or more episodes. On long-running series a storyliner plans the plots and writers turn them into scripts. In commercials the script usually comes from the advertising agency, where a [copywriter](/production-roles/copywriter/) and a creative team write it, not a screenwriter.
