The script says a ferry sinks in a storm. The production cannot afford a real ferry, a real storm or a tank big enough to fake both. The VFX supervisor sits down with the director and breaks the scene into pieces. Actors on a section of deck built on a gimbal, real water splashed from the side, a digital ferry and a digital sea added later. The VFX supervisor is the person responsible for how the visual effects look and how they will be made.

## What a VFX supervisor does

Visual effects (VFX) are images created or changed on a computer after the shoot. That covers anything from removing a modern road sign to building a whole city. The VFX supervisor is the creative and technical lead for that work and answers to the [director](/production-roles/director/) and the [producer](/production-roles/producer/). It often starts in [development](/how-a-film-is-made/#development) or early pre-production, when the script is broken down into effects shots and the team decides what is done on camera and what is done digitally.

On set the VFX supervisor makes sure the material will work in post. They advise the [director of photography](/production-roles/director-of-photography/) on green screens and camera moves, record measurements and lighting references, and coordinate with the [special effects supervisor](/production-roles/special-effects-supervisor/), whose effects (rain, fire, explosions) are real and happen on set. In post-production they brief the VFX vendors, review their work in regular sessions and decide when each shot is ready to show the director.

## Responsibilities

- Break down the script into VFX shots and propose how to achieve each one
- Advise the director on what is realistic within the budget and schedule
- Plan previsualisation, simple animated versions of complex sequences
- Supervise effects shots on set and gather the data the vendors need
- Help choose VFX vendors and brief them on the look
- Review and give notes on shots until they are approved
- Present finished shots to the director and producers for sign-off

## How the role touches the budget

Strongly, through decisions rather than bookkeeping. Each shot the VFX supervisor designs has a price, and a choice made on set (shoot a clean plate or not, use a real prop or a digital one) can add or save days of vendor work. The money itself is tracked by the [VFX producer](/production-roles/vfx-producer/). The two work as a pair, with the supervisor deciding what quality a shot needs and the producer checking what it costs and whether it fits.

## How people become VFX supervisors

Most come up through VFX companies. They start as artists, often in compositing (combining layers of images into one shot), then lead teams as VFX lead or VFX supervisor at a vendor. After years of that they move to the production side. Knowledge of cameras and lighting matters as much as software skills.

## In TV and commercials

On TV series the VFX supervisor handles many episodes at once with fixed delivery dates, so the work leans more on efficient methods and repeated set-ups. In commercials the VFX supervisor usually works for the post-production house and attends the shoot for one or two days, with the agency watching the work closely.
