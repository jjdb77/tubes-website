At ten past eight on a Sunday evening, the file for the next programme will not play. The playout operator sees the warning on a screen, switches to a backup copy and puts a trailer out in between. Viewers see a slightly longer break and nothing else. The playout operator is the person who keeps a channel on air, second by second.

## What a playout operator does

Playout is the last step before a programme reaches the viewer. Most channels today are played out by an automated system that follows a playlist built from the [scheduler](/production-roles/scheduler/)'s plan. Programmes, adverts, trailers, idents (the short clips that show the channel's logo) and subtitles all run from that playlist. The playout operator watches several channels at once in a transmission area, often called master control, checks that everything plays correctly and acts when it does not.

They also handle the moments that cannot be fully automated, such as switching to a live programme or an outside broadcast, joining a news update, or dropping an advert break when a live match overruns. Many operators also check incoming programme files before transmission, making sure they match the technical delivery specification and the right version has arrived.

## Responsibilities

- Monitor picture, sound and subtitles on one or more channels
- Check that programmes, adverts and trailers play as scheduled
- Switch to live sources and back again at the right moment
- Respond to faults and use backups so the channel stays on air
- Adjust the playlist when a live programme runs over or under
- Check incoming programme files against delivery specifications
- Log what went out and report incidents

## How the role touches the budget

Hardly at all. The playout operator does not handle money. They do see one cost effect of production, in the form of files that fail technical checks. A programme delivered in the wrong format or with faults goes back to the production company to fix, usually at its own cost. That is why [post-production supervisors](/production-roles/post-production-supervisor/) work to the broadcaster's delivery specification from the start of [delivery](/how-a-film-is-made/#delivery).

## How people get into the role

Many start as a transmission assistant or media operator, ingesting and checking files, and move to the playout desk after training. A technical interest, calm under pressure and comfort with shift work (channels run day and night) are what count. Some move on to broadcast engineering or operations management.

## At a playout company

Many broadcasters no longer run their own playout. They hire a specialist playout company that runs dozens of channels from one centre for several clients. The job is the same, only the operator works for the service provider rather than the broadcaster. In the US the role is usually called master control operator.
