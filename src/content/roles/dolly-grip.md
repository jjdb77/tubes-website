The camera has to glide slowly towards the actor and stop exactly as she turns her head. The dolly grip waits for the cue, pushes a few hundred kilos of camera and platform along a track, and lands on the mark within a centimetre. If the speed is wrong, the moment is gone. The dolly grip is the person who moves the camera on wheels.

## What a dolly grip does

A dolly is a wheeled platform for the camera, pushed along rails or across a smooth floor. Many dollies also have an arm that lifts the camera up and down during the shot. The dolly grip works under the [key grip](/production-roles/key-grip/) and alongside the [camera operator](/production-roles/camera-operator/).

They mostly work during the shoot. For each shot they lay and level the track, so the move is perfectly smooth, then rehearse with the operator and the [first assistant camera](/production-roles/first-assistant-camera/). The operator frames, the focus puller keeps it sharp and the dolly grip controls the speed and position of the move. The three need to work as one.

## Responsibilities

- Lay and level track for each dolly shot
- Push and steer the dolly, and operate the lifting arm
- Rehearse moves and agree marks with operator and focus puller
- Hit the same marks at the same speed in every take
- Maintain the dolly and track and keep them in working order
- Keep cast and crew safe around a moving camera

## How the role touches the budget

Barely. The dolly and track are part of the grip rental package. The dolly grip is paid a day or weekly rate with overtime on timesheets, and on smaller shoots one grip may do both dolly and general grip work.

## How people get into the role

Dolly grips usually come from the grip department, starting as grip and showing a feel for smooth movement and timing. Some specialise and work with the same operator or director of photography for years, because a good camera move depends on trust between them.

## On smaller productions

Smaller productions often use lighter sliders or handheld stabilisers instead of a full dolly. In that case there is no dedicated dolly grip, and a grip or the camera crew handles the move. In commercials and on large films, the dolly grip is a sought-after specialist.
