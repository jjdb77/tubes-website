Six weeks before delivery, the director adds a scene where the city skyline should be on fire. Four new shots, all complex. The VFX producer checks which vendor still has capacity, gets a bid within two days and finds that two simpler shots elsewhere can be cut to pay for it. The producer approves the swap the same afternoon. The VFX producer is the person who manages the money, the vendors and the schedule behind the visual effects.

## What a VFX producer does

The VFX producer works for the [producer](/production-roles/producer/) and the [line producer](/production-roles/line-producer/), side by side with the [VFX supervisor](/production-roles/vfx-supervisor/). Where the supervisor is responsible for how the shots look, the VFX producer is responsible for what they cost and when they arrive. Work starts in pre-production with a breakdown of the script into VFX shots and a first estimate of the VFX budget.

The VFX producer then sends the shots out for bids to one or more vendors, the companies that will make them. They negotiate prices and contracts, split the work between vendors and set the schedule. During [post-production](/how-a-film-is-made/#post-production) they track every shot, from turnover to final approval, and handle the inevitable changes as the edit moves.

## Responsibilities

- Break down the script with the VFX supervisor and estimate the VFX budget
- Prepare bid packages and request quotes from VFX vendors
- Negotiate vendor contracts, prices and payment schedules
- Keep the shot list, the master record of every VFX shot and its status
- Agree change orders when shots are added, cut or redesigned
- Report VFX costs and forecasts to the producers and [production accountant](/production-roles/production-accountant/)
- Make sure every final shot is delivered on time to the [post-production supervisor](/production-roles/post-production-supervisor/)

## How the role touches the budget

Directly and constantly. On films with many effects, VFX can be one of the largest lines below the line, and the VFX producer owns it. They compare each vendor bid against the budget, approve invoices, and keep a running forecast of what the remaining shots will cost. Shots are often added or changed after the edit, so the VFX producer has to know at any moment how much room is left and how much of the [contingency](/insights/how-much-contingency-does-a-production-need/) may be needed. The weekly [cost report](/insights/what-is-a-production-cost-report/) usually shows VFX as its own block.

## How people become VFX producers

Many start as coordinators at a VFX company, tracking shots and talking to clients, then become producers on the vendor side before moving to the production side. Others come from the production office or post-production coordination. Understanding how VFX work is made matters, because it is impossible to judge a bid without knowing what the artists actually do.

## In TV and commercials

On a TV series the VFX producer often manages a block of episodes, with the same vendors handling recurring shots, which makes per-episode budgets easier to forecast. In commercials the role usually sits at the post-production house, as the producer who quotes the job to the production company and the agency.
