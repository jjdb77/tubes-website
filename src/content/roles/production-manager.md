It is Thursday evening and the camera truck for Monday has been double booked by the rental house. By the time the crew wrap, the production manager has found a second supplier, agreed a price that fits the budget line, and told the camera team where to pick up the kit. Nobody on set noticed. The production manager is the person who runs the practical business of a production, day by day.

## What a production manager does

The [line producer](/production-roles/line-producer/) sets the budget and the overall plan. The production manager makes that plan work on the ground. They start in [pre-production](/how-a-film-is-made/#pre-production), usually a few weeks before the shoot, and set up the production office together with the [production coordinator](/production-roles/production-coordinator/). From then on they handle crew contracts, supplier deals, insurance paperwork, permits and the many small decisions that cost money.

During the shoot the production manager reads the daily progress report from the [first assistant director](/production-roles/first-assistant-director/), checks overtime, and signs off orders. On smaller productions there is no line producer, and the production manager does both jobs. In the US the title is often unit production manager (UPM), and on some European productions the role sits close to what others call the line producer.

## Responsibilities

- Hire crew below the heads of department and agree their rates and working hours
- Book equipment, vehicles, studios and other suppliers within the agreed budget lines
- Raise and approve purchase orders, the written orders that commit money before it is spent
- Make sure insurance, permits and safety paperwork are in place before each shoot day
- Track overtime and extra costs and report them to the line producer
- Work with the [location manager](/production-roles/location-manager/) and [transport captain](/production-roles/transport-captain/) on daily logistics
- Close down the production office and return all hired equipment at the end

## How the role touches the budget

Closely. The production manager does not write the budget, but most of the spending passes through their hands. Every purchase order, crew deal and rental agreement is a commitment against a budget line. They work with the [production accountant](/production-roles/production-accountant/) on the weekly [cost report](/insights/what-is-a-production-cost-report/) and explain why a line is over or under. A production manager who knows where the money is going can warn the line producer early, while there is still time to move it.

## How people become production managers

Most start as a [production assistant](/production-roles/production-assistant/) and move up through coordinator roles. Years in the production office teach what each department needs, what suppliers charge and which costs tend to grow. The next step after production manager is often line producer.

## In commercials

At a commercials production company the production manager often runs several jobs at once, each lasting a few days to a few weeks. They work with the [commercials producer](/production-roles/commercials-producer/), turn the approved quote into bookings, and make sure the final costs stay within what the client agreed to pay.
