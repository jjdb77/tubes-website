In the middle of a take the actor steps half a metre further than in rehearsal. The camera operator follows without a jerk, keeps the head in frame and lets the boom microphone stay just out of shot. Nobody says a word. The camera operator is the person whose hands and eye are on the camera while it rolls.

## What a camera operator does

The camera operator works for the [director of photography](/production-roles/director-of-photography/), who decides the look and the lighting. The operator turns each planned shot into a precise frame and movement. They often join a few days before the shoot to prepare the camera with the camera team, and then work every shooting day.

During rehearsals the operator watches the actors, agrees marks with the [first assistant camera](/production-roles/first-assistant-camera/) and timing with the [dolly grip](/production-roles/dolly-grip/). During the take they control the pan and tilt (turning the camera left and right, up and down) by hand, with wheels or with a remote head on a crane. After each take they tell the [director](/production-roles/director/) honestly whether the shot worked, because they are the only person who saw the frame through the eyepiece in real time.

## Responsibilities

- Frame and move the camera during rehearsals and takes
- Agree movement, timing and marks with focus puller and dolly grip
- Keep unwanted things out of frame, such as microphones, lights and crew
- Report problems with a take (a bump, a missed frame, a reflection) straight away
- Suggest framing options to the director of photography and the director
- Operate special rigs, such as a handheld rig, a camera stabiliser or a remote head
- Keep the camera position safe for cast and crew

## How the role touches the budget

No budget responsibility, and little influence on cost. The operator is paid a day or weekly rate and may ask for specific kit, such as a stabiliser rig or a particular fluid head, which appears as a rental line in the camera budget. Overtime of the camera crew is recorded on timesheets and checked by the production office.

## How people get into the role

The usual route goes through the camera department, from trainee to [second assistant camera](/production-roles/second-assistant-camera/) and focus puller, then to operator. Some operators specialise in one technique, such as handheld or a camera stabiliser, and are hired for that skill. Many DoPs operate on smaller productions and keep doing so on larger ones.

## In TV and commercials

On many smaller productions and in documentary there is no separate operator. The DoP or a single camera person frames the shots. In multi-camera TV studios, camera operators follow instructions from the [studio director](/production-roles/studio-director/) through headsets, and the director cuts between cameras live.
