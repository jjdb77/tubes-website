The director wants a famous rock song over the opening titles. The music supervisor asks the owners what it costs, and the answer is roughly the whole music budget. Over the next week they suggest three alternatives that sound like the same era, one by a lesser-known band that is happy to be in the film. The director picks one and the rest of the budget is safe. The music supervisor is the person who chooses and clears the existing music in a film.

## What a music supervisor does

Most films use a mix of original music from the [composer](/production-roles/composer/) and existing songs or recordings. To use an existing track, a production needs two kinds of permission. One covers the composition (the song itself), the other covers the specific recording. Each may belong to a different owner. The music supervisor finds the right music for the [director](/production-roles/director/), checks who owns it and negotiates the licences for the [producer](/production-roles/producer/).

They often start in pre-production, especially when actors sing or dance to music on set, because those tracks must be cleared before the shoot. Most of the work comes in [post-production](/how-a-film-is-made/#post-production), when the [editor](/production-roles/editor/) cuts with temporary music and the team decides what can stay, what must be replaced and what the composer will write instead.

## Responsibilities

- Discuss the musical style with the director and suggest songs that fit
- Clear music that is played or performed on set, before the shoot
- Find out who owns each composition and recording
- Negotiate licence fees and terms, such as territory, media and duration
- Keep the music budget and track every agreed fee
- Help hire the composer and agree the terms of their deal
- Prepare the music cue sheet, the list of every piece of music used, for [delivery](/how-a-film-is-made/#delivery)

## How the role touches the budget

Directly. The music supervisor usually manages the music budget line, made up of licence fees, the composer's deal and their own fee. A licence that covers only one country or a limited time is cheaper, but can cause problems later when a distributor needs worldwide rights. So the music supervisor balances today's cost against what the sales plan needs, together with the producer and the [production lawyer](/production-roles/production-lawyer/).

## How people get into the role

Many music supervisors come from the music industry, from record labels, music publishers or licensing companies. Others start as assistants to an established music supervisor or as music coordinators on productions. Knowing who owns what and having good contacts with labels and publishers is the core of the job.

## In TV and commercials

TV series often use many licensed songs per episode, so the music supervisor works on a steady weekly rhythm and the deals are more standardised. At a broadcaster, the [rights and clearances manager](/production-roles/rights-and-clearances-manager/) checks that music in delivered programmes is properly cleared. In commercials, music for one advertising campaign is often licensed for a limited period, and the fees for a famous song can be high.
