"Coming to two. Ready three, wide. Take three." Inside a dark room full of screens, the studio director talks without pause while a band plays its last chorus on a live entertainment show. Five cameras, a presenter waiting to walk on and an advert break in forty seconds. The studio director is the person who decides, shot by shot, what the audience at home sees.

## What a studio director does

Studio programmes are filmed with several cameras at the same time and cut live, or as if live. The studio director sits in the gallery (the control room next to the studio) with the [vision mixer](/production-roles/vision-mixer/), the producer and the sound and lighting teams. Watching a wall of monitors, one for each camera, they tell the [camera operators](/production-roles/camera-operator/) which shots to find and tell the vision mixer when to cut between them. On the studio floor, the [floor manager](/production-roles/floor-manager/) is their voice to the presenter, guests and audience.

The work starts well before the show. For music, entertainment and drama in a studio, the director writes a camera script that lists every shot and which camera takes it. They plan the set with the designer, agree positions for cameras and lights, and run rehearsals. For news, sport and chat shows there is less script and more reacting to what happens.

## Responsibilities

- Plan the visual style and camera positions for the programme
- Write a camera script or shot plan where the format allows
- Run rehearsals with cameras, presenters and performers
- Call shots and cuts during recording or transmission
- Keep the programme to time with the producer and the gallery team
- Work with sound, lighting and graphics so everything lands on cue
- Decide on retakes or pick-ups in recorded programmes

## How the role touches the budget

Indirectly. A studio is booked by the day, so rehearsal time, the number of cameras and the length of the recording all cost money. The director's plan decides how many cameras and operators are needed, whether a crane or a remote camera is hired, and how long the studio has to be booked. The production manager budgets for those choices and the director works within them. The director's own fee is usually a fixed amount per show or per series.

## How people become studio directors

Many started as camera operators, vision mixers or floor managers and learned the gallery from the inside. Others came through a broadcaster's trainee scheme or from directing smaller live events and online streams. Being calm under pressure and thinking several shots ahead are the skills that matter most.

## Compared with a film director

A [film director](/production-roles/director/) usually shoots one camera set-up at a time and shapes the result later in the edit. A studio director makes the edit in real time. In the US the job is simply called director, and the vision mixer is called the technical director.
