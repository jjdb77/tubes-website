The shoot wrapped on Friday. On Monday the distributor asks whether the picture can be ready a month earlier for a festival. The post-production supervisor opens the post schedule, checks which grading suite is free, how many visual effects shots are still open and whether the sound mix can move. By lunchtime the producer has an honest answer and a price. The post-production supervisor is the person who manages everything that happens to a film after the cameras stop.

## What a post-production supervisor does

Post-production is the stage where the footage becomes a finished film. It covers the edit, visual effects, colour, sound, music and the files that broadcasters and distributors need. The post-production supervisor plans that whole stretch and keeps it moving. They work for the [producer](/production-roles/producer/) and alongside the [line producer](/production-roles/line-producer/), and often start in [pre-production](/how-a-film-is-made/#pre-production), because choices made before the shoot (camera format, file workflow, where the editing team sits) decide what post will cost.

During the shoot they make sure footage reaches the cutting room safely every day. Once filming ends they draw up the schedule for picture lock, the moment the edit is final, and everything that follows it. They book facilities, hire post crew, and track every cost against the post budget until the last file is accepted in [delivery](/how-a-film-is-made/#delivery).

## Responsibilities

- Design the post workflow before the shoot, from camera files to final masters
- Build and maintain the post-production schedule and budget
- Hire or book the [editor](/production-roles/editor/), sound team, [colourist](/production-roles/colourist/) and post facilities
- Coordinate with the [VFX producer](/production-roles/vfx-producer/) on shot counts, bids and deadlines
- Organise screenings for the producers, financiers and broadcaster, and track their notes
- Manage the delivery list, the long set of files and documents a distributor or broadcaster requires
- Check the final masters pass technical quality control before they are sent

## How the role touches the budget

Directly. The post budget is a large block below the line (see [above the line vs below the line](/insights/above-the-line-vs-below-the-line/)), and the post-production supervisor owns it in practice. They collect quotes from facilities, approve invoices and report to the [production accountant](/production-roles/production-accountant/) on what is spent and what is still to come. A late picture lock or extra visual effects shots can move the numbers quickly, so they keep the estimate for completion of post up to date every week.

## How people get into the role

Many start as a runner in a post-production house or as an [assistant editor](/production-roles/assistant-editor/), then become post coordinator before taking on whole projects. That route teaches both the technical side (formats, file transfers, quality control) and how facilities price their work. Some come from the production office instead.

## In TV and commercials

On a TV series the post-production supervisor runs several episodes at once, each at a different stage, so the schedule becomes a rolling pipeline. Broadcasters have strict technical specifications and delivery dates fixed in the commission. In commercials the post producer at the production company or the post house does much the same work, on a far shorter timeline.
