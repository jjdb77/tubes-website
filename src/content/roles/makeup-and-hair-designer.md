The story jumps forty years between two scenes, and both are shot on the same day. In the morning the actor is twenty-five, by the afternoon he is sixty-five with thinning grey hair, liver spots and a scar from a fight that happened in between. The make-up and hair designer planned every step of that change weeks ago. They are the person responsible for how faces and hair look throughout a production.

## What a make-up and hair designer does

The designer works for the [director](/production-roles/director/) and the [producer](/production-roles/producer/) and starts in [pre-production](/how-a-film-is-made/#pre-production). They break down the script per character and scene, research the period, and design each look. Tests are done on camera with the [director of photography](/production-roles/director-of-photography/), because lights and lenses change how skin and hair read on screen. Looks are agreed with the [costume designer](/production-roles/costume-designer/) so that clothes, hair and make-up belong together.

During the shoot they lead a team of make-up artists and hairstylists who work in the make-up trailer from early morning, and stand by on set for touch-ups between takes. They keep photos and notes for continuity. When the story needs wounds, ageing or creatures, they work with prosthetics specialists, and sometimes with the [VFX supervisor](/production-roles/vfx-supervisor/) when part of the effect is finished digitally.

## Responsibilities

- Break down the script into looks per character and scene
- Design make-up and hair for the cast, including wigs and facial hair
- Run camera tests and get approval from the director
- Hire and lead make-up artists and hairstylists
- Plan the make-up schedule with the [second assistant director](/production-roles/second-assistant-director/), so each actor is ready on time
- Keep continuity records for every look
- Arrange prosthetics, blood and special make-up with specialists
- Manage the department budget for products, wigs, prosthetics and crew

## How the role touches the budget

The designer manages a department budget for products, wigs, prosthetics and the team's hours. Wigs and prosthetic pieces can be expensive and take weeks to make, so they are decided early. Make-up time also affects the schedule, which affects the whole budget. An actor who needs three hours in the chair has to be called early, and a complex look on a crowd scene means extra artists for the day.

## How people become make-up and hair designers

Most train in make-up, hairdressing or both, and then work as trainees and make-up artists on productions for years before leading a department. Some specialise in prosthetics or period hair along the way. Experience in theatre, television and fashion work is common.

## Titles across the industry

In the UK the title is usually make-up and hair designer or hair and make-up designer. In the US the work is often split between a make-up department head and a hair department head. On commercials and smaller TV shows one make-up artist may do both hair and make-up without a separate design role.
